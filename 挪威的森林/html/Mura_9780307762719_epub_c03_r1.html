村上春树 · Haruki Murakami
1
每周的漫步
Weekend Walks · Walking, Walking, Walking
漫步 日常

NAOKO CALLED ME THE FOLLOWING SATURDAY, AND THAT SUNDAY we had a date. I suppose I can call it a date. I can't think of a better word for it.

直子在下个周六打来电话，那个周日我们约了一次会。我想可以称之为约会吧——我想不出更好的词了。

As before, we walked the streets. We stopped someplace for coffee, walked some more, had supper in the evening, and said good-bye. Again, she talked only in snatches, but this didn't seem to bother her, and I made no special effort to keep the conversation going. We talked about whatever came to mind—our daily routines, our colleges; each a little fragment that led nowhere. We said nothing at all about the past. And mainly, we walked—and walked, and walked. Fortunately, Tokyo is such a big city, we could never have covered it all.

和上次一样，我们走在街上。停下来喝杯咖啡，再走一会儿，傍晚吃晚饭，然后告别。她还是只断断续续地说话，但她似乎并不介意，我也没刻意让对话继续。我们想到什么就聊什么——每天的日常、各自的大学——都是些没有下文的碎片。关于过去，我们只字不提。而主要的，就是走——不停地走。幸运的是东京这么大，我们永远不可能走完所有地方。

We kept on walking like this almost every weekend. She would lead, and I would follow close behind. Naoko had a variety of barrettes and always wore them with her right ear exposed. I remember her most clearly this way, from the back. She would toy with her barrette whenever she felt embarrassed by something. And she was always dabbing at her mouth with a handkerchief. She did this whenever she had something to say. The more I observed these habits of hers, the more I came to like her.

几乎每个周末我们都这样走。她在前面领路，我紧随其后。直子有很多种发夹，总是把右耳露出来。我最记得的就是这个背影。每当她感到不好意思时，就会摆弄发夹。想说点什么的时候，她就用手帕轻轻擦嘴角。我观察她的这些小习惯越久，就越喜欢她。

2
直子的新生活
Naoko's New Life · A Girls' College, A Simple Room
生活 人物

Naoko went to a girls' college on the rural western edge of Tokyo, a nice little school famous for its teaching of English. Nearby was a narrow irrigation canal with clean, clear water, and Naoko and I would often walk along its banks. Sometimes she would invite me up to her apartment and cook for me. It never seemed to concern her that the two of us were in such close quarters together. The room was small and neat and so lacking in frills that only the stockings drying in the corner by the window gave any hint that a girl lived there. She led a spare, simple life with hardly any friends. No one who had known her in high school could have imagined her like this. Back then, she had dressed with real flair and surrounded herself with a million friends. When I saw her room, I realized that, like me, she had wanted to go away to college and begin a new life far from anyone she knew.

直子就读于东京西郊乡下一所女子大学，一所以英语教学闻名的不错的小学校。附近有一条清澈的灌溉水渠，我和直子常常沿着渠边散步。有时她会邀我去她的公寓，做饭给我吃。她似乎毫不在意我们两人待在这么小的空间里。房间小而整洁，没有任何多余的装饰，只有窗边晾着的丝袜暗示着这里住着一个女孩。她过着清简的生活，几乎没有朋友。任何认识高中时的她的人都无法想象她会变成这样。那时她打扮得很有品位，身边围满了朋友。看到她的房间，我明白了——和我一样，她想要到远方上大学，在一个谁都不认识的地方开始新生活。

"Know why I chose this school?" she said with a smile. "Because nobody from home was coming here. We were all supposed to go someplace more chic. You know what I mean."

"知道我为什么选这所学校吗？"她笑着说，"因为老家没有一个人会来这儿。大家本该去更时髦的地方。你明白我的意思。"

My relationship with Naoko was not without its progress, though. Little by little, she grew more accustomed to me, and I to her. When summer vacation ended and the new term started, Naoko began walking next to me as if it were the most natural thing in the world to do. She saw me as a friend now, I concluded, and walking side by side with such a beautiful girl was by no means painful for me. We kept walking all over Tokyo in the same undirected way, climbing hills, crossing rivers and rail lines, just walking and walking with no destination in mind. We forged straight ahead, as if our walking were a religious ritual meant to heal our wounded spirits. If it rained, we used umbrellas, but in any case we walked.

我和直子的关系并非没有进展。她渐渐习惯了我，我也习惯了她。暑假结束新学期开始后，直子开始走在我身旁，仿佛这是世界上最自然不过的事。我得出了结论——她现在视我为朋友，而和这么漂亮的女孩并肩走路，对我来说绝不是件痛苦的事。我们继续这样漫无目的地走遍东京，爬山、过河、跨越铁道线，只是走啊走，没有目的地。我们一直向前，仿佛走路是一种治愈受伤灵魂的宗教仪式。下雨时就撑伞，但无论如何，我们还是走。

Then came autumn, and the dormitory grounds were buried in zelkova leaves. The fragrance of a new season arrived when I put on my first sweater. Having worn out one pair of shoes, I bought some new ones, of suede.

秋天到了，宿舍的院子里铺满了榉树叶。穿上第一件毛衣时，新季节的气息到来了。走坏了一双鞋，我又买了一双新的——麂皮的。

I can't seem to recall what we talked about then. Nothing special, I would guess. We continued to avoid any mention of the past and rarely mentioned Kizuki. We could face each other over coffee cups in total silence.

我似乎已记不起那时我们聊了什么。大概也没什么特别的。我们继续回避谈论过去，也几乎不提木月。我们可以面对咖啡杯完全沉默地坐着。

3
突击队员的笑话
Storm Trooper Jokes · Making Naoko Laugh
喜剧 回忆

Naoko liked to hear me tell stories about Storm Trooper. Once he had a date with a classmate (a girl in geography, of course) but came back in the early evening looking glum. "Tell me, Wa-Wa-Watanabe, what do you talk about with gir-gir-girls?" I don't remember how I answered him, but he had picked the wrong guy to ask.

直子喜欢听我讲突击队员的故事。有一次他和班上一个女生约会（当然是地理系的），傍晚回来时垂头丧气。"告——告诉我，渡——渡——渡边，你和女——女——女生聊什么？"我忘了我当时怎么回答的，但他找错了人问。

My stories of Storm Trooper always made Naoko laugh. Not many things succeeded in doing that, so I talked about him often, though I was not exactly proud of myself for using him this way. He just happened to be the youngest son in a not-too-wealthy family who had grown up a little too serious for his own good. Making maps was the one small dream of his one small life. Who had the right to make fun of him for that?

我的突击队员故事总能让直子笑。能让她笑的事情本来不多，所以我经常讲起他，虽然我并不为此感到自豪。他只是不太富裕家庭里最小的儿子，从小过于认真。画地图是他小小生命中唯一的小梦想。谁有资格拿这个取笑他呢？

By then, however, "Storm Trooper jokes" had become an indispensable source of dormitory talk, and there was no way for me to undo what I had done. Besides, the sight of Naoko's smiling face had become my own special source of pleasure. I went on supplying everyone with new stories.

但那时，"突击队员笑话"已经成为宿舍里不可或缺的谈资，我也无法挽回自己造成的局面。况且，看到直子的笑脸已经成了我自己的快乐源泉。我继续为大家提供新的故事。

4
心中的硬核
The Hard Kernel · Can I Really Love?
内心 告白

Naoko asked me one time—just once—if I had a girl I liked. I told her about the one I had left behind in Kobe. "She was nice," I said, "I enjoyed sleeping with her, and I miss her every now and then, but finally, she didn't move me. I don't know, sometimes I think I've got this hard kernel in my heart, and nothing much can get inside it. I doubt if I can really love anybody."

直子问过我一次——只有一次——问我有没有喜欢的女孩。我向她提起留在神户的那个女孩。"她挺好的，"我说，"我跟她睡过，偶尔也会想念她，但说到底，她没有打动我。不知道怎么说——有时候我觉得自己心里有个硬核，没什么东西能进到里面去。我怀疑自己是否真的能爱一个人。"

"Have you ever been in love?" Naoko asked.

"你爱过谁吗？"直子问。

"Never," I said.

"从来没有。"我说。

She didn't ask me more than that.

她没有再问下去。

✦ 内心的壁垒

"我心里有个硬核，没什么东西能进到里面去。"——渡边对自己情感能力的早期诊断，也是贯穿全书的自我认知。

5
冬日漫步
Winter Walks · Clinging Arms, Searching Eyes
季节 孤独

When autumn ended and cold winds began tearing through the city, Naoko would often walk pressed against my arm. I could sense her breathing through the thick cloth of her duffle coat. She would entwine her arm with mine, or cram her hand in my pocket, or, when it was really cold, cling tightly to my arm, shivering. None of this had any special meaning. I just kept walking with my hands shoved in my pockets. Our rubber-soled shoes made hardly any sound on the pavement, except for the dry crackling when we trod the broad, withered leaves of sycamore on the roads. I felt sorry for Naoko whenever I heard that sound. My arm was not the one she needed, but the arm of someone else. My warmth was not what she needed, but the warmth of someone else. I felt almost guilty being me.

秋天结束，冷风开始撕扯城市时，直子常常紧贴着我的手臂走路。我能隔着粗呢大衣感受到她的呼吸。她会勾住我的手臂，或者把手塞进我的口袋，天特别冷时就紧紧抱住我的胳膊发抖。这一切没有任何特别的意义。我只是继续两手插在口袋里走着。我们的胶底鞋在路面上几乎没有声响，只有踩到宽阔的梧桐枯叶时发出干裂的脆响。每当听到那声音，我都为直子感到难过。我的手臂不是她需要的，而是另外某个人的手臂。我的温暖不是她需要的，而是另外某个人的温暖。我几乎为自己是我自己而感到愧疚。

As the winter deepened, the transparent clarity of Naoko's eyes seemed to increase. It was a clarity that had nowhere to go. Sometimes Naoko would lock her eyes on mine for no apparent reason. She seemed to be searching for something and this would give me a strange, lonely, helpless sort of feeling.

随着冬天渐深，直子眼中那种透明的清澈似乎更浓了。那是一种无处可去的清澈。有时直子会无缘无故地凝视着我的眼睛。她似乎在寻找什么，这让我感到一种奇怪的、孤独的、无助的感觉。

I wondered if she was trying to convey something to me, something she could not put into words—something prior to words that she could not grasp within herself and which therefore had no hope of ever turning into words. Instead, she would fiddle with her barrette, dab at the corners of her mouth with a handkerchief, or look into my eyes in that meaningless way. I wanted to hold her tight when she did these things, but I would hesitate and hold back. I was afraid I might hurt her if I did that. And so the two of us kept walking the streets of Tokyo, Naoko searching for words in space.

我不知道她是否想向我传达什么——某种无法用语言表达的东西，某种先于语言存在、连她自己也把握不住、因而永远无法转化为语言的东西。取而代之的是，她摆弄发夹，用手帕擦拭嘴角，或者那种无意义地凝视我的眼睛。每当这时我都想紧紧抱住她，但我犹豫了，克制住了。我怕那样会伤害她。于是我们继续走在东京的街头——直子在虚空中寻找着词句。

6
失语症
Word-Searching Sickness · Saturday Nights Alone
孤独 日常

The guys in the dorm would always razz me when I got a call from Naoko or went out on a Sunday morning. They assumed, naturally enough, that I had found a girlfriend. There was no way to explain the truth to them, and no need to explain it, so I let them think what they wanted to.

宿舍的伙计们每次看到我接到直子的电话或周日早上出门，总要起哄。他们当然以为我找到了女朋友。我没法向他们解释实情，也没有必要，所以随他们想去。

AND SO I WENT from eighteen to nineteen. Each day the sun would rise and set, the flag would be raised and lowered. Each Sunday I would have a date with my dead friend's girl. I had no idea what I was doing or what I was going to do. For my courses I would read Claudel and Racine and Eisenstein, but they meant almost nothing to me. I made no friends in classes, and hardly knew anyone in the dorm. The others in the dorm thought I wanted to be a writer, because I was always alone with a book, but I had no such ambition. There was nothing I wanted to be.

就这样我从十八岁变成了十九岁。每天太阳升起又落下，旗帜升起又降下。每个周日和我死去的朋友的女孩约会。我不知道自己在做什么，也不知道自己将要做什么。课上读克洛岱尔、拉辛、爱森斯坦，但他们几乎对我毫无意义。我在班里没交到朋友，宿舍里也几乎不认识谁。宿舍里的人以为我想当作家，因为我总是一个人拿着书——但我没有那样的抱负。我没有任何想要成为的东西。

I tried to talk about this feeling with Naoko. She, at least, would be able to understand what I was feeling with some degree of precision, I thought. But I could never find the words to express myself. Strange, I seemed to have caught her word-searching sickness.

我试着和直子谈这种感觉。我想她至少能比较准确地理解我的感受。但我就是找不到表达自己的语言。奇怪——我好像传染了她的"失语症"。

On Saturday nights I would sit by the phone in the lobby, waiting for Naoko to call. Most of the others were out on Saturday nights, so the lobby was usually deserted. I would stare at the grains of light suspended in that silent space, struggling to see into my own heart. What did I want? And what did others want from me? But I could never find the answers. Sometimes I would reach out and try to grasp the grains of light, but my fingers touched nothing.

周六晚上，我会坐在大厅的电话旁，等直子打来。周六晚上大多数人都出去了，大厅通常空无一人。我凝视着那静默空间中悬浮的光粒，努力看清自己的内心。我想要什么？别人又想从我这里得到什么？但永远找不到答案。有时我伸出手，想要抓住那些光粒，但手指什么也触碰不到。

I READ A LOT, but not a lot of different books: I like to read my favorites again and again. Back then it was Truman Capote, John Updike, Scott Fitzgerald, Raymond Chandler, but I didn't see anyone else in my classes or the dorm reading writers like that. They liked Kazumi Takahashi, Kenzaburo Oe, Yukio Mishima, or contemporary French novelists, which was another reason I didn't have much to say to anybody but kept to myself and my books. With my eyes closed, I would touch a familiar book and draw its fragrance deep inside me. This was enough to make me happy.

我读很多书，但并不是读很多不同的书：我喜欢反复读自己最喜欢的那些。那时是杜鲁门·卡波特、约翰·厄普代克、斯科特·菲茨杰拉德、雷蒙德·钱德勒——但班里和宿舍里我没见别人读这类作家。他们喜欢高桥和己、大江健三郎、三岛由纪夫或当代法国小说家，这也是我和谁都没什么好说的另一个原因——我把自己关在书里。闭上眼睛，触摸一本熟悉的书，把它的香气深深吸入体内——这就足以让我快乐。

7
遇见永泽
Meeting Nagasawa · The Great Gatsby Connection
人物 友谊

When I did finally meet the one person in my world who had read Gatsby, he and I became friends because of it. His name was Nagasawa. He was two years older than I, and because he was majoring in legal studies at the prestigious Tokyo University, he was on the fast track to national leadership. We lived in the same dorm and knew each other only by sight, until one day when I was reading Gatsby in a sunny spot in the dining hall. He sat down next to me and asked what I was reading. When I told him, he asked if I was enjoying it. "This is my third time through," I said, "and every time I find something new that I like even more than the last time."

当我终于在这个世界上遇到了唯一一个读过《盖茨比》的人时，我们因此成了朋友。他叫永泽，比我大两岁，在著名的东京大学读法律，是通往国家领导层的快车道。我们住在同一栋宿舍，只是脸熟而已。直到有一天，我在食堂的阳光下看《盖茨比》，他坐在我旁边，问我在读什么。我告诉他之后，他问我觉得这本书怎么样。"这是我第三遍读了，"我说，"每一次都能发现新的喜欢的东西，比上一次更喜欢。"

"This man says he has read The Great Gatsby three times," he said as if to himself. "Well, any friend of Gatsby is a friend of mine."

"这个人说他把《了不起的盖茨比》读了三遍。"他像是自言自语地说，"嗯，盖茨比的朋友就是我的朋友。"

And so we became friends. This happened in October.

我们就这么成了朋友。那是十月的事。

8
永泽的哲学
Nagasawa's Philosophy · Dead Authors & Gentlemen
哲学 人物

The better I got to know Nagasawa, the stranger he seemed. I had met a lot of strange people in my day, but none as strange as Nagasawa. He was a far more voracious reader than I, but he made it a rule never to touch a book by any author who had not been dead at least thirty years. "That's the only kind of book I can trust," he said.

我越了解永泽，越觉得他古怪。我见过很多怪人，但没有一个像永泽这么怪。他读书比我还多，但他给自己定了个规矩：绝不碰任何去世未满三十年的作家写的书。"这是唯一可以信任的书。"他说。

"It's not that I don't believe in contemporary literature," he added, "but I don't want to waste valuable time reading any book that has not had the baptism of time. Life is too short."

"倒不是我不相信当代文学，"他补充道，"但我不想把宝贵的时间浪费在没有经过时间洗礼的书上。人生太短暂了。"

"What kind of authors do you like?" I asked, speaking in respectful tones to this man two years my senior.

"你喜欢哪些作家？"我用敬重的语气问这位年长我两岁的人。

"Balzac, Dante, Joseph Conrad, Dickens," he answered without hesitation.

"巴尔扎克、但丁、约瑟夫·康拉德、狄更斯。"他毫不犹豫地回答。

"Not exactly fashionable."

"不算时髦。"

"That's why I read them. If you only read the books that everyone else is reading, you can only think what everyone else is thinking. That's the world of hicks and slobs. Real people would be ashamed of themselves doing that. Haven't you noticed, Watanabe? You and I are the only real ones in the dorm. The other guys are crap."

"所以我读它们。如果你只读别人都在读的书，你就只能想别人都在想的事。那是乡下人和蠢货的世界。真正的人会为自己那么做感到羞耻。你没有注意到吗，渡边？你我才是宿舍里唯二真实的人。其他人都是一坨屎。"

No one else in the dorm knew that Nagasawa was a secret reader of classic novels, nor would it have mattered if they had. Nagasawa was known for being smart. He breezed into Tokyo University, he got good grades, he would take the Civil Service Exam, join the Foreign Ministry, and become a diplomat. He came from a super family.

宿舍里没人知道永泽是偷偷读经典小说的秘密读者，即便知道也无所谓。永泽以聪明著称。他轻松考入东京大学，成绩优异——他要考公务员，进外务省，成为外交官。他出身于超级家庭。

Nagasawa had a certain inborn quality that drew people to him and made them follow him. He knew how to stand at the head of the pack, to assess the situation, to give precise and tactful instructions that others would obey. Above his head hung an aura that revealed his powers like an angel's halo, the mere sight of which would inspire awe in people for this superior being. Which is why it shocked everyone that Nagasawa chose me, a person with no distinctive qualities, to be his special friend. People I hardly knew treated me with a certain respect because of it, but what they did not seem to realize was that the reason for my having been chosen was a simple one, namely that I treated Nagasawa with none of the adulation he received from other people. I had a definite interest in the strange, complex aspects of his nature, but none of those other things—his grades, his aura, his looks—impressed me. This must have been something new for him.

永泽有一种与生俱来的气质，能把人吸引到身边，让他们追随他。他知道如何站在人群前列，审时度势，发出精确而巧妙的指令让人服从。他头顶环绕着一种光环，如同天使的光轮，仅凭其显现就足以让人对这个高人敬畏不已。正因如此，当永泽选择了我这个毫无特长的人作为特别的朋友时，所有人都震惊了。我几乎不认识的人都因此对我有几分敬意，但他们似乎没有意识到，我被选中的原因很简单——我对永泽没有其他人那种阿谀奉承。我对他人性中奇怪而复杂的方面有明确的兴趣，但其他那些——他的成绩、他的光环、他的外貌——都打动不了我。这对他来说一定是新鲜事。

There were sides to Nagasawa's personality that conflicted in the extreme. Even I would be moved by his kindness at times, but he could, just as easily, be malicious and cruel. He was both a spirit of amazing loftiness and an irredeemable man of the gutter. He could charge forward, the optimistic leader, even as his heart writhed in a swamp of loneliness. I saw these paradoxical qualities of his from the start, and I could never understand why they weren't just as obvious to everyone else. He lived in his own special hell.

我从地上捡起衣服穿上。衬衫前襟还是湿的，冰凉，带着直子的气味。我在桌上的便签上写道："等你平静下来后，我想和你好好谈谈。请尽快给我电话。生日快乐。"我最后看了一眼直子的肩膀，走出门外，轻轻关上了门。

A WEEK WENT BY, but no call came. Naoko's apartment house had no system for summoning people to the phone, and so on Sunday morning I took the train out to Kokubunji. She was not there, and the name had been removed from her door. The windows and storm shutters were closed up tight. The manager told me that Naoko had moved out three days earlier. Where she had moved to, he had no idea.

一周过去了，没有电话。直子的公寓没有叫人接电话的系统，于是周日早上我坐火车去了国分寺。她不在，门上的名牌也被摘掉了。窗户和防雨窗紧闭。管理员告诉我，直子三天前搬走了。搬到哪里去了，他不知道。

I went back to the dorm and wrote a long letter addressed to Naoko at her home in Kobe. Wherever she was, they would forward it to her at least.

我回到宿舍，写了一封长信，寄到直子在神户的家。不管她在哪里，至少家人会把信转给她。

"I probably should not have done what I did, and yet I believe that it was all I could do. The warmth and closeness I felt for you at that moment was something I had never experienced before. I need you to answer this letter. Whatever that answer may be, I need to have it."

"我大概不该做那件事，但我相信那是我唯一能做的事。那一刻我感受到的温暖和亲密，是我从未经历过的。我需要你回信。无论答案是什么，我都需要它。"

The answer did not come.

回信没有来。

✦ 无声的消失

直子搬走了，没有留下任何地址。渡边的信寄往神户，但回音全无。"无论答案是什么，我都需要它。"——但答案没有来。

夏天的空白
The Summer Void · Strikes, Trucks, and Waiting
虚无 时代

Something inside me had dropped away, and nothing came in to fill the cavern. There was an abnormal lightness to my body, and sounds had a hollow echo to them. I went to classes more faithfully than ever. The lectures were boring, and I never talked to my classmates, but I had nothing else to do. I would sit by myself in the very front row of the lecture hall, speak to no one, and eat alone. I quit smoking.

我体内有什么东西掉落消失了，没有东西来填充那个空洞。我的身体异常轻盈，声音带着空洞的回响。我比以往更老实地去上课。讲座很无聊，我从不和同学说话，但我也没有别的事可做。我一个人坐在讲堂最前排，不和任何人说话，独自吃饭。我戒了烟。

The student strike started at the end of May. "Dismantle the university," they all screamed. Go ahead, do it, I thought. Dismantle it. Tear it apart. Crush it to bits. I don't give a damn. A breath of fresh air for me. I'm ready for anything. I'll help if you need it. Just go ahead and do it.

学生罢课始于五月底。"拆毁大学！"他们全都大喊着。好啊，动手吧，我想。拆了它、撕了它、碾碎它。我他妈才不在乎。对我来说是新鲜空气。我准备好了，随时奉陪。需要帮忙的话我可以搭把手。尽管干吧。

With the campus blockaded and lectures suspended, I started to work at a trucking company. Riding shotgun, loading and unloading trucks, that kind of stuff. It was tougher than I thought. At first I could hardly get out of bed in the morning with the pain. The money was good, though, and as long as I kept my body moving I could forget about the emptiness inside.

校园被封锁、课程停摆后，我开始在一家货运公司干活。押车、装货卸货之类的。比我想象的累多了。刚开始几天早上疼得几乎起不了床。但钱还不错，而且只要让身体动起来，就能忘记内心的空虚。

In June I wrote Naoko another long letter, addressing it again to her house in Kobe. It said pretty much the same thing as the first letter, but at the end I added this: "Waiting for your answer is one of the most painful things I have ever been through. At least let me know whether or not I hurt you." When I dropped it in the mail, I felt as if the cavern inside me had grown again.

六月，我又写了一封长信给直子，同样寄到神户她家。内容几乎和第一封一样，但末尾我加了一句："等待你的回答是我经历过的最痛苦的事之一。至少让我知道我有没有伤害到你。"把信投进邮筒时，我感到内心的空洞又变大了。

Also during June I went out with Nagasawa twice again to sleep with girls. It was easy both times. … What the hell am I doing? I started wondering as soon as I was alone and feeling disgusted with myself. And yet it was all I could do. My body was hungering for women. All the time I was sleeping with those girls, I thought about Naoko, about the white shape of her naked body in the darkness, her sighs, the sound of the rain. The more I thought about these things, the hungrier my body grew. I went up to the roof with my whiskey and asked myself where I thought I was heading.

六月里我又跟永泽出去玩了两次女孩。两次都很容易。……我他妈到底在干什么？一独处我就开始这么想，感到自我厌恶。但这是我唯一能做的事。我的肉体渴望女人。和那些女孩睡觉时，我满脑子都是直子——黑暗中她裸体的白色轮廓、她的叹息、雨声。想得越多，肉体就越饥渴。我拿着威士忌爬上屋顶，问自己到底要往哪里去。

Finally, at the beginning of July, a letter came from Naoko. A short letter.

终于，七月初，直子的信来了。一封短信。

Please forgive me for not answering sooner. But try to understand. It took me a very long time before I was in any condition to write, and I have started this letter at least ten times. Writing is a painful process for me.

请原谅我没有早点回信。但请理解。过了很久我才恢复到能够写信的状态，这封信我至少起了十次头。写信对我来说是一个痛苦的过程。

Let me begin with my conclusion. I have decided to take a year off from school. Officially, it's a leave of absence, but I suspect that I will never be going back. This will no doubt come as a surprise to you, but in fact I had been thinking about doing this for a very long time. I tried a few times to mention it to you, but I was never able to make myself begin. I was afraid even to pronounce the words.

先说结论吧。我决定休学一年。正式来说是休学，但我怀疑自己不会再回去了。这肯定让你吃惊，但实际上我考虑这件事已经很久了。我几次想跟你提起，但始终没能说出口。我甚至害怕说出那几个字。

Try not to be so worked up about things. Whatever happened—or didn't happen—the end result would have been the same. This may not be the best way to put it, and I'm sorry if it hurts you. What I am trying to tell you is, I don't want you to blame yourself for what happened with me. It is something I have to take on all by myself.

别太激动了。不管发生了什么——或没发生什么——结果都是一样的。这样说也许不太合适，如果伤害了你我很抱歉。我想告诉你的是，不要因为那晚发生的事而自责。那是必须由我一个人承担的事。

After I moved out of my apartment, I came back to my family's house in Kobe and was seeing a doctor for a while. He tells me there is a place in the hills outside Kyoto that would be perfect for me, and I'm thinking of spending a little time there. It's not exactly a hospital, more a sanatorium kind of thing with a far freer style of treatment. I'll leave the details for another letter.

搬出公寓后，我回到了神户的家里，看了一段时间医生。医生告诉我，京都郊外的山里有一个地方很适合我，我打算去那里住一阵子。不算是医院，更像疗养院，治疗方式自由得多。细节下次再写。

I read Naoko's letter again and again, and each time I read it I would be filled with that same unbearable sadness I used to feel whenever Naoko herself stared into my eyes. I had no way to deal with it, no place I could take it to or hide it away. Like the wind passing over my body, it had neither shape nor weight, nor could I wrap myself in it.

我把直子的信读了一遍又一遍，每一次读，心中就涌起那种无法承受的悲伤——就像从前直子凝视我的眼睛时我感受到的一样。我不知如何应对它，无处可带它去，也无处可藏。它像风一样吹过我的身体，没有形状也没有重量，我无法将自己包裹其中。

萤火虫
The Firefly · A Trail of Light in the Darkness
象征 结局

AT THE END OF THE MONTH, Storm Trooper gave me a firefly. It was in an instant coffee jar with air holes in the lid and containing some blades of grass and a little water. In the bright room the firefly looked like an ordinary black bug you'd find by a pond somewhere, but Storm Trooper insisted that it was the real thing.

月底，突击队员给了我一只萤火虫。装在一个速溶咖啡瓶里，盖子上扎了透气孔，里面放了几片草叶和一点水。明亮的房间里，萤火虫看起来就像池塘边常见的黑色小虫，但突击队员坚持说这是货真价实的萤火虫。

After dark the dorm was hushed, like a ruin. The flag had been lowered and the lights glowed in the windows of the dining hall. With so few students left, they turned on only half the lights in the place, keeping the right half dark and the left half lighted. Still, the smell of dinner drifted up to us—some kind of cream stew.

天黑后，宿舍寂静如废墟。旗已降下，食堂的窗户透出灯光。学生太少，只开了半边灯——右半边暗着，左半边亮着。晚饭的香气飘了上来——某种奶油炖菜。

I took my bottled firefly to the roof. No one else was up there. A white undershirt hung on a clothesline where someone had forgotten to take it in, waving in the evening breeze like the discarded shell of some huge insect. I climbed a steel ladder in the corner of the roof to the top of the dormitory's water tank.

我把装萤火虫的瓶子带上屋顶。上面没有人。晾衣绳上挂着有人忘了收的白汗衫，在晚风中飘动，像某种巨大昆虫蜕下的壳。我从屋顶角落的铁梯爬上了宿舍楼的水箱顶。

The firefly made a faint glow in the bottom of the jar, its light too weak, its color too pale. I hadn't seen a firefly in years, but the ones in my memory sent a far more intense light into the summer darkness, and that brilliant, burning image was the one that had stayed with me all that time.

萤火虫在瓶底发出微弱的光，太弱了，颜色太淡了。我多年没见过萤火虫了，但记忆中的萤火虫向夏夜的黑暗中投射出远为强烈的光——那个鲜明炽热的形象一直留在我心里。

Maybe this firefly was on the verge of death. I gave the jar a few shakes. The firefly bumped against the glass walls and tried to fly, but its light remained dim.

也许这只萤火虫已经垂死了。我摇了摇瓶子。萤火虫撞到玻璃壁上，试图飞起来，但它的光依然暗淡。

I closed my eyes and steeped myself in that long-ago darkness. I heard the wind with unusual clarity. Far from strong, the wind swept past me, leaving strangely brilliant trails in the darkness. I opened my eyes to find the darkness of the summer night a few degrees deeper than it had been.

我闭上眼睛，沉浸在那遥远的黑暗中。我异常清晰地听到了风声。风并不强劲，它从我身边掠过，在黑暗中留下奇异而鲜明的轨迹。我睁开眼睛，发现夏夜的黑暗比刚才又浓了几度。

I twisted open the lid of the jar and took the firefly out, setting it on the two-inch lip of the water tank. It seemed not to grasp its new surroundings. It hobbled around the head of a steel bolt, catching its legs on curling scabs of paint. It moved to the right until it found its way blocked, then circled back to the left. Finally, with some effort, it mounted the head of the bolt and crouched there for a while, unmoving, as if it had taken its last breath.

我拧开瓶盖，取出萤火虫，放在水箱两寸宽的边缘上。它似乎没有弄清自己的新环境。它跌跌撞撞地爬过一个螺栓头，腿被翻卷的油漆片绊住。它往右爬，碰到阻碍，又绕回左边。最后，它吃力地攀上螺栓头，蜷在那里一动不动，像是断了气。

Still leaning against the handrail, I studied the firefly. Neither I nor it made a move for a very long time. The wind continued sweeping past the two of us while the numberless leaves of the zelkova tree rustled in the darkness.

我仍然靠在栏杆上，观察着那只萤火虫。我和它都很久没有动。风持续从我们两人身边掠过，无数榉树叶在黑暗中沙沙作响。

I waited forever.

我等了很久很久。

Only much later did the firefly take to the air. As if some thought had suddenly come to it, the firefly spread its wings, and in a moment it had flown past the handrail to float in the pale darkness. It traced a swift arc by the side of the water tank as if trying to bring back a lost interval in time. And then, after hovering there for a few seconds as if to watch its curved line of light blend into the wind, it finally flew off to the east.

又过了很久，萤火虫才飞了起来。仿佛突然想到了什么，它展开翅膀，瞬间飞过栏杆，漂浮在浅淡的黑暗中。它在水箱旁边迅速画出一道弧线，仿佛要唤回某个失去的时间片段。然后，在空中悬停了几秒，像是在目送自己那弯曲的光线融入风中，它终于向东飞去了。

Long after the firefly had disappeared, the trail of its light remained inside me, its pale, faint glow hovering on and on in the thick darkness behind my eyelids like a lost soul.

萤火虫消失很久之后，它光的轨迹依然留在我体内。那淡淡的微光在我眼睑后浓重的黑暗中久久悬浮，像一个迷失的灵魂。

More than once I tried stretching my hand out in that darkness. My fingers touched nothing. The faint glow remained, just beyond their grasp.

我不止一次在那黑暗中伸出手去。手指什么也碰不到。那微光还在，就在手指尖稍稍够不着的地方。

✦ 全章最核心的意象

萤火虫微弱的光——如同直子，如同渡边心中的空洞。它飞走了，但光的轨迹依然留在眼睑后的黑暗中。伸出手去，什么也触碰不到。
