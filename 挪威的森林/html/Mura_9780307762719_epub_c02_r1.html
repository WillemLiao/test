1
学生宿舍
The Dormitory · 1968–1970
场景 背景

ONCE UPON A TIME, MANY YEARS AGO—JUST TWENTY YEARS AGO, in fact—I was living in a dormitory. I was eighteen and a freshman. I was new to Tokyo and new to living alone, and so my anxious parents found a private dorm for me to live in rather than the kind of single room that most students took.

很久很久以前——其实也就是二十年以前——我住在学生宿舍里。我十八岁，大学一年级。我刚到东京，第一次独自生活，于是放心不下的父母为我找了一家私人宿舍，而不是大多数学生住的那种单人公寓。

Located on a hill with open views in the middle of the city, the dormitory compound sat on a large quadrangle surrounded by a concrete wall. A huge, towering zelkova tree stood just inside the front gate. People said it was at least a hundred and fifty years old. Standing at its base, you could look up and see nothing of the sky through its dense cover of green leaves.

宿舍坐落在一座可以远眺的开阔山丘上，宽阔的方形院落四周环绕着水泥围墙。正门内有一棵高大的榉树，据说至少有一百五十年树龄。站在树下仰头，浓密的绿叶遮得看不见一丝天空。

There was just one problem with the place: its political smell. The complex was run by some kind of fishy foundation that centered on some kind of extreme right-wing guy. One thing was certain: in the dorm complex there existed a privileged club composed of elite students from various universities.

这个地方只有一个问题：它的政治气味。宿舍由一个可疑的财团运营，核心人物是个极右派。可以确定的是，宿舍里存在一个由各大学精英学生组成的特权俱乐部。

✦ 宿舍的基调

1968年春至1970年春，"我"在这座"可疑的"宿舍住了两年。不管右翼左翼，对日常生活而言都无所谓。

2
升旗仪式
Flag Ceremony · Nakano & Uniform
场景 讽刺

Each day at the complex began with the solemn raising of the flag. They played the national anthem, too, of course. You can't have one without the other.

每一天，宿舍都以庄严的升旗仪式开始。当然还有国歌。有其一必有其二。

The head of the east dormitory was in charge of the flag. He was a tall, eagle-eyed man in his late fifties or early sixties. His bristly hair was flecked with gray, and his sunburned neck bore a long scar. People whispered that he was a graduate of the wartime Nakano spy school. Next to him stood a student who acted as his assistant. No one really knew this guy, either. He had the world's shortest crewcut and always wore a navy blue student uniform. His nickname was "Uniform."

东宿舍的管理员负责升旗。他身材高大，目光如鹰，五十七八或六十出头。花白的板寸，晒黑的脖子上有一道长疤。有人私下说他是战时中野间谍学校的毕业生。他旁边站着一个学生助手。没人知道这个人的来历。他留着世上最短的板寸，永远穿着藏青色学生制服，绰号叫"制服"。

This creepy couple would raise the banner of the Rising Sun every morning at six. Uniform held a ceremonial box of unfinished paulownia wood, while Nakano carried a Sony tape player at his side. Nakano set the player at the base of the flagpole. Uniform opened the box to reveal a neatly folded banner. This he reverentially proffered to Nakano, who would clip it to the rope on the flagpole, revealing the bright red circle of the rising sun on a field of pure white. Then Uniform pressed the switch for the playing of the anthem.

这对诡异的搭档每天早上六点升起太阳旗。制服捧着素木桐 ceremonial 盒，中野提着索尼录音机。中野把录音机放在旗杆底座上。制服打开盒子，里面是一面叠得整整齐齐的旗。他恭恭敬敬地递交给中野，中野将旗夹在旗杆的绳索上——纯白底色上鲜红的日轮。然后制服按下播放键。

"May Our Lord's Reign…" And up the flag would climb. "Until pebbles turn to boulders…" It would reach halfway up the pole. "And be covered with moss." Now it was at the top.

"愿我主统治……"旗升上去。"直到卵石变成巨岩……"升到半空。"长满青苔。"升到杆顶。

I did not know why the flag had to be taken down at night. The nation continued to exist after dark, and plenty of people worked the whole night through—track construction crews and taxi drivers and bar hostesses and firemen and night watchmen: it seemed unfair to me that such people were denied the protection of the flag.

我不知道为什么晚上要把旗降下来。国家在日落后继续存在，有很多人彻夜工作——铺轨工、出租车司机、酒吧女招待、消防员、守夜人：我觉得这些人被剥夺了国旗的庇护，这不公平。

✦ 荒诞的仪式

每日升旗降旗，从无间断。"我"的冷眼旁观为宿舍的怪诞氛围定调。

3
房间与室友 "Storm Trooper"
The Room · a morgue-clean room, a map lover
人物 描写

The filth of these all-male rooms was horrifying. Moldy mandarin orange skins clung to the bottoms of wastebaskets. Any wind that blew through would raise clouds of dust.

这些纯男性房间的肮脏程度令人作呕。腐败的橘子皮粘在垃圾桶底，一阵风吹过便扬起灰蒙蒙的尘土。

My room, on the other hand, was as sanitary as a morgue. The floor and window were spotless, the mattresses were aired each week, all pencils stood in the pencil holders, and even the curtains were laundered once a month. My roommate was clean crazy.

而我的房间，干净得像太平间。地板和窗户一尘不染，每周晒一次褥子，铅笔都插在笔筒里，连窗帘都每月洗一次。我的室友有洁癖。

"Storm Trooper" was majoring in geography at a national university. "I'm studying muh-muh-maps." When I told him I was majoring in drama: "I decided to come to Tokyo and get my parents to se-send me money so I could study muh-muh-maps. But not you, huh?"

"突击队员"在一所国立大学学地理。"我在学地——地图。"我说我学戏剧。"我来东京让父母寄钱学地——地图。你不一样，嗯？"

He was 100-percent indifferent to politics. He wore a uniform because he didn't want to be bothered choosing clothes. What interested him were things like changes in the coastline or the completion of a new rail tunnel. Nothing else.

他对政治百分之百不关心。他穿制服是因为不想为挑衣服费神。让他感兴趣的只有海岸线的变化和新铁路隧道的贯通之类的。其他一概不关心。

✦ 室友的荒诞

外表极右、内心纯真的地图爱好者。他唯一的口吃只出现在说"地图"的时候——每次都口吃。

4
广播体操风波
Radio Calisthenics · the jumping that shook the bed
喜剧 冲突

He was up at six each morning with the strains of "May Our Lord's Reign." He'd get dressed, wash his face—forever. Then Radio Calisthenics with the rest of the nation.

他每天早上六点随着"愿我主统治"的旋律起床，洗脸——洗个没完。然后和全国人民一起做广播体操。

I was used to reading late at night and sleeping until eight. I stood it for three days, but by the morning of the fourth day, I couldn't take it anymore. "Hey, can you do that on the roof or someplace? I can't sleep." "But it's already six-thirty!"

我习惯晚睡晚起。忍了三天，第四天早上终于受不了了。"喂，你能到屋顶上做吗？我睡不着。""可已经六点半了！"

The problem was the jumping. He took his jumping seriously and made the bed bounce every time he hit the floor. When I asked him to cut the jumping: "Juh-jumping? What's that?" He didn't even know he was doing it.

问题是跳跃。他跳得极其认真，每次落地都让床铺震动。我叫他别跳了："跳——跳？什么跳？"他甚至不知道自己做了跳跃动作。

"I can't leave anything out. I've been doing the same thing every day for ten years, and once I start I do the whole routine unconsciously."

"我不能漏掉任何一节。十年来我每天都做同一套动作，一旦开始就会无意识地做完整套。"

"Gee, Watanabe, why don't you just get up and exercise with me?"

"喂，渡边，不如你跟我一起起来做操吧。"

✦ 困惑的善意

Storm Trooper 完全无法理解有人不需要六点半起床做操——他的逻辑里没有"选择"。

5
与直子重逢
Reunion · the Chuo Line, Yotsuya
重逢 对话

Naoko chuckled when I told her the story of Storm Trooper and his Radio Calisthenics. I hadn't been trying to amuse her, but I ended up laughing myself. Though her smile vanished in an instant, I enjoyed seeing it for the first time in a long while.

直子听我讲"突击队员"和广播体操的故事时微微一笑。我并非有意逗她，但自己也跟着笑了出来。虽然她的笑容转瞬即逝，但我很高兴——很久没见她笑了。

We had left the train at Yotsuya and were walking along the embankment by the station. It was a Sunday afternoon in the middle of May. The cherry trees' brilliant green leaves stirred in the air and splashed sunlight in all directions. This was an early summer day.

我们在四谷下了电车，沿着车站旁的护堤散步。五月中旬的一个周日下午。樱树鲜亮的绿叶在风中摇曳，向四周溅洒着阳光。初夏的日子。

Almost a year had gone by since I had last seen Naoko, and in that time she had lost so much weight as to look like a different person. And a lot prettier than I remembered. I wanted to tell her that, but couldn't find a good way to put it.

距离上次见到直子已经快一年了，这期间她瘦了很多，看起来像变了个人。而且比我记忆中漂亮多了。我想告诉她，却不知如何开口。

We had not planned to meet but had run into each other on the Chuo commuter line. She had suggested that we leave the train, which we happened to do in Yotsuya. Alone together, we had nothing in particular to talk about, and I wasn't quite sure why Naoko had suggested we get off the train.

我们并非约好见面，而是在中央线上偶然碰到的。她提议下车，我们就碰巧在四谷出来了。两个人单独在一起，也没什么特别的话要说，我也不大确定直子为什么要提议下车。

✦ 偶然的重逢

木月死后一年，两人在电车上偶遇。她瘦了，也更漂亮了。"我"跟在她身后一码处走着。

6
长距离步行
The Long Walk · Yotsuya to Komagome
步行 对话

This was no mere stroll for Naoko. She turned right at Iidabashi, came out at the moat, crossed the intersection at Jinbocho, climbed the hill at Ochanomizu, and came out at Hongo. From there she followed the trolley tracks to Komagome. It was a challenging route.

但对直子来说这绝非简单的散步。她在饭田桥右转，走到护城河边，穿过神保町十字路口，爬上御茶水的坡，来到本乡。然后沿着电车轨道一直走到驹込。这是条很累人的路线。

We went to a shop by the station for a bowl of noodles. "Well, you're in good shape." "Surprised?" "You bet." "I was a long-distance runner in junior high. I've always had strong legs."

我们在车站旁的面店里吃面。"体力不错嘛。""吃惊了？""当然。""告诉你，我初中时是长跑选手。我的腿一直很结实。"

She was playing with the ashtray on the table. "I wonder… if you wouldn't mind… Do you think we could see each other again? I know I don't have any right to be asking you this."

她摆弄着桌上的烟灰缸。"那个……你要是不介意……我们还能再见面吗？我知道我没资格这么问你。"

"I can never say what I want to say. I try to say something, but all I get are the wrong words—the wrong words or the exact opposite words from what I mean. I try to correct myself, and that only makes it worse. I lose track of what I was trying to say to begin with. It's like I'm split in two and playing tag with myself."

"我总是说不出口。想说什么却只能说出相反的话。想纠正，越说越糟。最后连自己原本想说什么都忘了。就像身体分成两半，在互相捉迷藏似的。"

✦ 语言的分裂

直子第一次向"我"透露自己的困境：话到嘴边却变成相反的意思。这是她内心分裂的早期信号。

7
初识直子 · 木月的世界
First Meeting · Kizuki, the irreplaceable host
回忆 人物

I FIRST MET NAOKO in the spring of my second year of high school. She was the girlfriend of my best (and only) friend, Kizuki. The two of them had been close almost from birth.

我第一次见到直子是高二那年春天。她是我的最好（也是唯一）朋友木月的女朋友。两人从出生起就很要好。

Kizuki and Naoko and I: odd, but that was the most comfortable combination. Introducing a fourth person into the mix would always make things a little awkward. We were like a TV talk show, with me the guest, Kizuki the talented host, and Naoko his assistant.

木月、直子和我——奇怪，但这是最舒服的组合。再加入第四个人总是会让气氛变得微妙。我们就像一档电视访谈节目，我是嘉宾，木月是才华横溢的主持人，直子是助理主持。

And yet he was not the least bit sociable. I was his only real friend at school. I could never understand why such a smart and capable talker did not turn his talents to the broader world around him but remained satisfied to concentrate on our little trio. Nor could I understand why he picked me to be his friend.

但他一点都不善于社交。我是他在学校唯一真正的朋友。我始终不明白，这么聪明、这么会说话的人，为什么不把才华施展到更广阔的世界中去，而满足于专注于我们这个小三人组。我也不明白他为什么选择我做朋友。

The three of us spent a lot of time together, but whenever Kizuki left the room, Naoko and I had trouble talking to each other.

我们三个人在一起的时间很多，但只要木月一离开房间，直子和我就不知该说什么了。

✦ 不可替代的木月

三人组合的平衡依赖于木月——他一离开，剩下两人便陷入沉默。这为后来的断裂埋下伏笔。

8
木月的死
Kizuki's Death · the N-360, white flowers on the desk
死亡 转折

Naoko might have been angry with me because I, and not she, had been the last one to see Kizuki alive.

直子之所以生我的气，也许是因为我——而不是她——是最后一个见到木月活着的人。

It had been a nice afternoon in May. After lunch, Kizuki suggested we cut classes and go play pool. When I won the first game, he got serious and won the other three. "Why so serious?" "I didn't want to lose today," said Kizuki with a satisfied smile.

那是五月一个晴朗的下午。午饭后木月提议翘课去打台球。我赢了第一局后他认真起来，赢了后面三局。"怎么这么认真？""我今天不想输。"木月满意地笑着说。

He died that night in his garage. He led a rubber hose from the exhaust pipe of his N-360 to a window, taped over the gap, and revved the engine. His parents found him when they opened the garage to put their car away. His radio was going, and a gas station receipt was tucked under the windshield wiper.

那晚他在自家车库死了。他把橡胶管从N-360的排气管接到车窗上，用胶带封住缝隙，发动了引擎。父母打开车库准备停车时发现了他。收音机还开着，雨刷下夹着一张加油站的收据。

In the ten months between Kizuki's death and graduation, I was unable to find a place for myself in the world around me. I started sleeping with one of the girls at school, but that didn't last six months. I applied to a private university in Tokyo, and I passed without exhilaration. I wanted to begin a new life where I didn't know a soul.

木月死后到毕业的十个月里，我在周围的世界中找不到自己的位置。我和学校里一个女生睡了，但没持续六个月。我报考了一所东京的私立大学，毫无激动地考上了。我想在一个一个人也不认识的地方开始新生活。

✦ 十七岁的死亡

"我"是最后一个见到木月活着的人。"为什么这么认真？""我今天不想输。"——这是木月最后的、隐喻般的话语。

9
死亡作为生命的一部分
Death as Part of Life · the knot of air
哲学 存在

There was only one thing for me to do: stop taking everything so seriously; establish a proper distance between myself and everything else. I tried hard to forget, but there remained inside me a vague knot-of-air kind of thing.

我只有一件事可做：不再对一切那么认真；在自己和一切之间建立适当的距离。我拼命想忘记，但心里始终残留着一团模糊的空气疙瘩。

Death exists, not as the opposite but as a part of life.

死不是生的对立面，而是生的一部分。

Translated into words, it's a cliché, but at the time I felt it not as words but as that knot of air inside me. Death exists—in a paperweight, in four red and white balls on a billiard table—and we go on living and breathing it into our lungs like fine dust.

用语言说出来是陈词滥调，但当时我感觉到的不是语言，而是胸中的那个空气疙瘩。死亡存在于纸镇中、存在于台球桌上四颗红白球中——我们继续活着，把它像细微的尘埃一样吸入肺中。

The night Kizuki died, I lost the ability to see death in such simple terms. Death was not the opposite of life. It was already here, within my being, it had always been here, and no struggle would permit me to forget that. When it took the seventeen-year-old Kizuki that night in May, death took me as well.

木月死去的那个夜晚，我失去了将死亡视为单纯对立面的能力。死不是生的对立面。它已经在这里，在我的存在之中，它一直都在这里，无论我如何挣扎都无法忘记。当那个五月的夜晚死带走了十七岁的木月，死也带走了我。

✦ 全书最核心的命题

"死不是生的对立面，而是生的一部分。"——这一认知不是来自书本，而是来自木月之死留在胸中的"空气疙瘩"。
