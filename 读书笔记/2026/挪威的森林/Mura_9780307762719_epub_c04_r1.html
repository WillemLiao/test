1
学潮之后
After the Strike · Riot Police, Roll Call, and Emptiness
时代 幻灭

DURING SUMMER BREAK THE UNIVERSITY CALLED IN THE RIOT police, who broke down the barricades and arrested the students inside. This was nothing special. It's what all the schools were doing at the time. The universities were not so easily "dismantled." Massive amounts of capital had been invested in them, and they were not about to dissolve just because a few students had gone wild.

暑假期间，大学叫来了防暴警察，他们拆除了路障，逮捕了里面的学生。这没什么特别——当时所有学校都在这么做。大学没有那么容易被"拆毁"。大量的资本投资在其中，它们不会因为几个学生胡闹就解散。

I went to the campus in September expecting to find rubble. The place was untouched. The library's books had not been carted off, the professors' offices had not been destroyed, the student affairs office had not been burned to the ground. I was thunderstruck. What the hell had those guys been doing behind the barricades?

九月我回到校园，以为会看到一片废墟。但一切完好如初。图书馆的书没有被运走，教授办公室没有被破坏，学生事务处没有被烧成平地。我惊呆了——那些家伙他妈的躲在路障后面到底干什么了？

When the strike was defused and lectures started up again under police occupation, the first ones to take their seats in the classrooms were those assholes who had led the strike. As if nothing had ever happened, they sat there taking notes and answering "here" when roll was called. I found this incredible.

当罢课被化解、在警察占领下重新开始上课时，最先在教室里坐下的就是那些领导罢课的混蛋。好像什么都没发生过一样，他们坐在那里记笔记，点名时回答"到"。我觉得这难以置信。

Hey, Kizuki, I thought, you're not missing a damn thing. This world is a piece of shit. The assholes are earning their college credits and helping to create a society in their own disgusting image.

喂，木月，我想，你什么也没错过。这个世界就是一堆狗屎。那些混蛋正在赚取大学学分，帮助创造一个以他们自己那恶心的形象为蓝本的社会。

For a while I attended classes but refused to answer when they called the roll. I knew it was a pointless gesture, but I felt so bad I had no choice. All I managed to do was to isolate myself more than ever from my classmates. By remaining silent when my name was called, I made everyone uncomfortable for a few seconds.

有一阵子我去上课，但点名时不回答。我知道这是毫无意义的姿态，但我感觉太糟了，别无选择。我唯一做到的就是比以前更加孤立自己。当我的名字被点到时保持沉默，让所有人都尴尬几秒钟。

By the second week in September I reached the conclusion that a college education was meaningless. I decided to think of it as a period of training in techniques for dealing with boredom.

到九月第二周，我得出结论：大学教育毫无意义。我决定把它视为一段训练如何应对无聊的时期。

Meanwhile, Storm Trooper had vanished. His stuff was gone, his name tag removed. The dorm head said only, "He's withdrawn from the dormitory. You'll be alone in the room for the time being." I couldn't get him to tell me why Storm Trooper had disappeared.

与此同时，突击队员消失了。他的东西不见了，门上的名牌被摘掉了。管理员只说："他退宿了。你暂时一个人住这间房。"我没法让他说出突击队员消失的原因。

Storm Trooper's iceberg poster stayed on the wall for a time, but I eventually took it down and replaced it with Jim Morrison and Miles Davis. This made the room seem a little more like my own. I used some of the money I had saved from work to buy a small stereo. At night I would drink alone and listen to music. I thought about Storm Trooper every now and then, but I enjoyed living alone.

突击队员的冰山海报在墙上挂了一阵，但最终我把它换成了吉姆·莫里森和迈尔斯·戴维斯。这让房间更像是我自己的了。我用打工攒的钱买了台小音响。夜里我独自喝酒听音乐。偶尔会想起突击队员，但我喜欢独自生活。

防暴警察、虚伪的学生领袖、消失了的朋友。渡边以沉默应对点名——这所大学的一切都让他感到荒谬。

2
遇见绿子
Meeting Midori · "Euripides. Electra."
相遇 对话

AT ELEVEN-THIRTY one Monday, after a lecture on Euripides in History of Drama, I took a ten-minute walk to a little restaurant and had an omelette and salad for lunch. The place was on a quiet back street and it had somewhat higher prices than the student dining hall, but you could relax there, and they knew how to make a good omelette.

周一十一点半，戏剧史课上完欧里庇得斯后，我走了十分钟到一家小餐馆吃午饭——煎蛋卷和沙拉。餐馆在僻静的后街上，比学生食堂贵一些，但环境放松，煎蛋卷也做得好。

Before long I noticed that one of the girls kept glancing in my direction. She had extremely short hair and wore dark sunglasses and a white cotton minidress. I had no idea who she was, so I went on with my lunch, but she soon slipped out of her seat and came over to where I was sitting. With one hand on the edge of my table, she said, "You're Watanabe, aren't you?"

不久我注意到一个女孩不停地朝我这边看。她头发极短，戴着深色太阳镜，穿着白色棉质迷你裙。我不认识她，于是继续吃午饭，但她很快从座位上溜出来，走到我桌旁，一只手扶着桌沿说："你是渡边，对吧？"

"Euripides," she said. "Electra. 'No god hearkens to my helpless cry.' You know—the class just ended."

"欧里庇得斯，"她说，"《厄勒克特拉》。'没有神倾听我无助的哭喊。'你知道吧——刚下课。"

I stared at her hard. She took off her sunglasses. At last I remembered her—a freshman I had seen in History of Drama. A striking change in hairstyle had kept me from recognizing her.

我仔细盯着她。她摘下墨镜。我终于想起来了——戏剧史课上见过的一个新生。发型变化太大，我没认出来。

"Oh," I said, touching a spot a few inches below my shoulder, "your hair was down to here before summer break."

"哦，"我说，在肩膀下几寸的地方比划了一下，"暑假前你的头发长到这儿。"

"You're right," she said. "I had a perm this summer, and it was just awful. I was ready to kill myself. I looked like a corpse on the beach with seaweed stuck to my head. So I figured as long as I was ready to die, I might as well cut it all off. At least it's cool in the summer." She ran her hand through her pixie cut and gave me a smile.

"你说得对，"她说，"夏天我烫了头发，难看得要死。我都不想活了——看起来像海滩上头顶海藻的尸体。我想反正都要死了，不如全剪掉。至少夏天凉快。"她用手抚过男孩般的短发，对我笑了笑。

"It looks good, though," I said, still munching on my omelette. "Let me see your profile."

"不过看起来挺好的，"我嚼着煎蛋卷说，"让我看看侧面。"

She turned away and held the pose for a few seconds.

她转过头，保持了几秒钟。

"Yeah, I thought so. It really looks good on you. Nicely shaped head. Pretty ears, too, uncovered like that."

"嗯，我就知道。真的很适合你。头型很好看。耳朵露出来也很漂亮。"

"So I'm not crazy after all! I thought I looked good myself once I cut it all off. Not one guy likes it, though. They all tell me I look like a first-grader or a concentration camp survivor. What's this thing that guys have for girls with long hair? Fascists, the whole bunch of them!"

"看来我没疯！我自己也觉得剪了之后很好看。但没有一个男生喜欢。他们都说我看起来像一年级小学生或集中营幸存者。男生到底怎么回事？全都喜欢长头发？法西斯！一帮法西斯！"

From the girl who sat before me now, though, surged a fresh and physical life force. She was like a small animal that has popped into the world with the coming of spring. Her eyes moved like an independent organism with joy, laughter, anger, amazement, and despair. I hadn't seen a face so vivid and expressive in ages, and I enjoyed watching it live and move.

然而，坐在我面前的这个女孩身上，涌动着一股新鲜的生命力。她像一只随着春天降临世界的小动物。她的眼睛像一个独立的有机体一样运动着——喜悦、大笑、愤怒、惊叹和绝望。我很久没有见过如此生动、如此富于表现力的面孔了，我喜欢看它运动和变化。

小林绿子——短发、墨镜、迷你裙。"像一只随着春天降临世界的小动物。"她以欧里庇得斯的台词作为自我介绍的方式。

3
绿子的幽默
Midori's Wit · Black Coffee, Green, and Bogart
对话 幽默

"You don't have to go back to them?" I asked, motioning toward her three companions.

"你不用回到他们那边去？"我朝她的三个同伴示意了一下。

"Nah. I'll go back when they serve the food. Am I interrupting your meal?"

"不用。等他们上菜了我再回去。我打扰你吃饭了吗？"

"There's nothing left to interrupt," I said, ordering coffee when she showed no sign of leaving.

"已经没什么可打扰的了。"我说，见她没有要走的意思，便点了咖啡。

"Now you tell me," she said. "Why didn't you answer today when they called the roll? You are Watanabe, aren't you? Toru Watanabe?"

"现在你告诉我，"她说，"今天点名你为什么不回答？你是渡边吧？渡边彻？"

"I just didn't feel like it today."

"今天就是不想回答。"

She took her sunglasses off again, set them on the table, and looked at me as if she were staring into the cage of some rare animal at the zoo. "'I just didn't feel like it today.' You talk like Humphrey Bogart. Cool. Tough."

她又摘下墨镜放在桌上，看着我，像是在动物园看珍稀动物的笼子。"'今天就是不想回答。'你说话像亨弗莱·鲍嘉。又酷又硬。"

"Don't be silly. I'm just an ordinary guy. Like everybody else."

"别傻了。我就是个普通人。和其他人一样。"

"Look at that. You drink it black."

"你看，你喝清咖啡。"

"It's got nothing to do with Humphrey Bogart," I explained patiently, "I just don't happen to like sweets. I think you've got me all wrong."

"这和亨弗莱·鲍嘉没关系，"我耐心解释，"我只是不喜欢甜的东西。你把我想错了。"

"Do you like green?"

"你喜欢绿色吗？"

"Why do you ask?"

"为什么这么问？"

"You're wearing a green polo shirt."

"你穿着绿色polo衫。"

"Not especially. I'll wear anything."

"没什么特别喜欢的。我什么都穿。"

"'Not especially. I'll wear anything.' I love the way you talk. Like spreading plaster nice and smooth. Has anybody ever told you that?"

"'没什么特别喜欢的。我什么都穿。'我爱死你说话的方式了。像抹墙灰一样光滑。有人告诉过你吗？"

"My name's Midori," she said. "'Green.' But green looks terrible on me. Weird, huh? It's like I'm cursed, don't you think? My sister's name is Momoko: 'Peach Girl.'"

"我叫Midori（绿子），她说，"就是'绿色'的意思。但绿色穿在我身上难看死了。怪吧？像是被诅咒了，你不觉得吗？我姐姐叫桃子。"

"Does she look good in pink?"

"她穿粉色好看吗？"

"She looks great in pink! She was born to wear pink. It's totally unfair."

"她穿粉好看极了！她生来就该穿粉色。太不公平了。"

✦ "绿子"之名

Midori = 绿色。因为名字叫绿，穿绿色却难看。姐姐叫桃子（Momoko），穿粉色好看——"太不公平了"。

4
欧里庇得斯被中断
Euripides Interrupted · Helmets, Handbills, Escape
政治 场景

I WENT TO THE FOLLOWING week's drama lecture, but still saw no sign of Midori Kobayashi. After a quick survey of the room convinced me she was not there, I took my usual seat in the front row and wrote a letter to Naoko while I waited for the professor to come.

接下来一周的戏剧课，我还是没看到小林绿子。快速扫视教室确认她不在后，我坐回第一排的老位子，一边等教授来，一边给直子写信。

The lecture was about half over and the professor was drawing a sketch of a Greek stage on the blackboard when the door opened again and two students in helmets walked in. They looked like some kind of comedy team, one tall and thin and pale, the other short and round and dark and totally unsuited to the long beard he wore. The tall one carried an armload of political agitation handbills. The short one walked up to the professor and said, with a degree of politeness, that they would like to use the second half of his period for political debate and hoped that he would cooperate.

课上了一半，教授正在黑板上画希腊舞台的草图，门又开了，两个戴头盔的学生走了进来。他们看起来像某种喜剧组合——一个又高又瘦又苍白，一个又矮又圆又黑，完全不适合他那把长胡子。高的抱着一摞政治煽动传单。矮的走到教授面前，略带礼貌地说，他们想用下半节课进行政治辩论，希望教授配合。

"Let's get out of here," said Midori.

"我们出去吧。"绿子说。

I nodded and stood, and the two of us made for the door. The round guy said something to me at that point, but I couldn't catch it. Midori waved to him and said, "See ya later."

我点点头站起来，两人朝门口走去。圆脸男对我说了句什么，我没听清。绿子朝他挥挥手说："回头见。"

"Gee, are we counterrevolutionaries?" Midori asked me when we were outside. "Are we going to be strung up on telephone poles if the revolution succeeds?"

"哎呀，我们是反革命吗？"出来后绿子问我，"革命成功了的话，我们会被吊在电线杆上吗？"

"Let's have lunch first, just in case."

"以防万一，先吃午饭吧。"

Midori took me to Yotsuya by bus and showed me to a fancy boxed-lunch specialty shop in a sheltered spot just behind the station. The minute we sat down they served us soup and the lunch of the day in square, red-lacquered boxes. This was a place worth a bus ride to eat at.

绿子带我坐巴士到四谷，带我去了车站后面一个隐蔽位置的高级盒饭店。刚一坐下，他们就上了汤和装在红色漆方盒里的当日午餐。这是个值得坐一趟巴士来吃的地方。

"Great food," I said.

"太好吃了。"我说。

"And cheap, too. I've been coming here since high school. My old school's right down the street. They were so strict, we had to sneak out to eat here. They'd suspend you if they caught you eating out."

"而且便宜。我从高中就常来。我的学校就在这条街上。学校管得很严，我们得偷溜出来吃。被抓到在校外吃饭要停学的。"

5
绿子的学校
Midori's School · Sanitary Napkins, Rich Girls, and Smoke
回忆 成长

SHE TOOK ME TO her old high school a short walk from Yotsuya.

她带我去了离四谷不远的一所高中——她的母校。

Midori Kobayashi and I sat on a park bench together, looking at the building where she used to go to high school. Ivy clung to the walls, and pigeons huddled beneath the gables, resting their wings. It was a nice old building with character. A great oak tree stood in the schoolyard, and a column of white smoke rose straight up beside it.

小林绿子和我一起坐在公园长椅上，望着她曾经就读的高中校舍。常春藤爬满了墙壁，鸽子聚集在屋檐下休憩翅膀。这是一栋很有韵味的老建筑。校园里有一棵大橡树，旁边一缕白烟笔直升起。

"Do you know what that smoke is?" Midori asked me without warning.

"你知道那是什么烟吗？"绿子突然问我。

"They're burning sanitary napkins."

"他们在烧卫生巾。"

"Sanitary napkins, tampons, stuff like that," Midori said with a smile. "It is a girls' school. The old janitor collects them from all the receptacles and burns them in the incinerator. That's the smoke."

"卫生巾、卫生棉条之类的，"绿子笑着说，"是女校嘛。老校工从各个收集箱收起来，拿到焚烧炉烧。那就是那烟。"

"I really didn't want to go to this school," Midori said. She gave her head a little shake. "I wanted to go to an absolutely ordinary public high school. An ordinary school with ordinary people where I could relax and have fun like an ordinary teenager. But my parents thought it would look good for me to go to this fancy place. They're the ones who stuck me in here."

"我真的不想上这所学校，"绿子说，轻轻摇了摇头，"我想去一所普普通通的公立高中。一所都是普通人的普通学校，可以像普通十几岁孩子一样放松、开心。但我父母觉得上这所高级学校对我有好处。是他们把我塞进来的。"

"You know, I've got certificates of merit for never having been late or missed a day of school. That's how much I hated the place. Get it?"

"你知道吗，我得了全勤奖——从来不迟到、不缺席。我就是这么恨这所学校。明白吗？"

I shook my head.

我摇了摇头。

"It's 'cause I hated the place so much. I wasn't going to let it beat me. I figured, let it get me once and I'd be finished. I was scared I'd just keep slipping down and down. I'd crawl to school with a temperature of a hundred and three. The teacher would ask me if I was sick, but I'd say no. At graduation they gave me certificates for perfect attendance and perfect punctuality, plus a French dictionary. That's why I'm taking German now. I didn't want to owe this school anything. I'm not kidding."

"因为我太恨这个地方了。我不能让它打败我。我想，如果被它打败一次我就完了。我害怕自己会一直滑下去、滑下去。发着高烧也要爬去上学。老师问我是不是病了，我说没有。毕业时他们给了我全勤奖和全时奖，还有一本法语词典。所以我现在才学德语。我不想欠这所学校任何东西。我没开玩笑。"

✦ 反叛的姿态

"恨它，所以绝不输给它。"——绿子的生存哲学：用全勤奖来报复自己讨厌的学校，用学德语来拒绝接受法语词典的馈赠。

6
小林书店
Kobayashi Bookstore · Magazines, Bonsai, and Dreams
家庭 背景

"I was the only one in the whole school who lived in a place like Kita-Otsuka Toshima. And under 'parent's profession' it said, 'bookstore owner.' Everybody in my class thought that was so neat: 'Oh, you're so lucky, you can read any book you like' and stuff. Of course, they were thinking of some monster bookstore like Kinokuniya. They could never have imagined the poor little Kobayashi Bookstore.

"全校只有我一个人住在北大冢那种地方。家长职业栏写的是'书店经营者'。全班同学都觉得好棒：'哇，你好幸运啊，什么书都能看！'他们当然想的是纪伊国屋那种超级书店。他们永远想象不到可怜的小小林书店是什么样子。

The door creaks open and you've got nothing but magazines. The steady sellers are the ladies' magazines with illustrated pullout sections on the latest sexual techniques. The local housewives buy them and sit at the kitchen table reading them from cover to cover, and give 'em a try when their husbands get home. And they've got the most incredible positions! Is this what housewives have on their minds all day? The cartoons are the other big seller: Magazine, Sunday, Jump. And of course the weeklies. So this 'bookstore' is almost all magazines.

门吱呀一声打开，里面全是杂志。畅销的是带插图性爱技巧特辑的妇女杂志。附近的家庭主妇买回家坐在厨房桌边从头读到尾，等老公回家了实践一下。那些姿势可了不得！主妇们整天就在想这些吗？漫画也是大卖品：《Magazine》《Sunday》《Jump》。还有周刊。所以这家'书店'几乎全是杂志。

Oh, there are a few books, paperbacks, like mysteries and swashbucklers and romances. That's all that sell. And how-to books: how to win at go, how to raise bonsai, how to give wedding speeches, how to have sex, how to quit smoking, you name it. We even sell writing supplies—stacks of ballpoint pens and pencils and notebooks next to the cash register. But that's it. No War and Peace, no Kenzaburo Oe, no Catcher in the Rye. That's the Kobayashi Bookstore. That's how 'lucky' I am. Do you think I'm lucky?"

哦，也有几本书，平装本——推理小说、武侠、爱情。只有这些卖得动。还有实用书：怎么赢围棋、怎么养盆景、怎么在婚礼上致辞、怎么做爱、怎么戒烟，应有尽有。我们还卖文具——收银机旁边堆着圆珠笔、铅笔和笔记本。但仅此而已。没有《战争与和平》，没有大江健三郎，没有《麦田里的守望者》。这就是小林书店。这就是我"幸运"的地方。你觉得我幸运吗？"

"You know what I mean. Everybody in the neighborhood comes there, some of them for years, and we deliver. It's a good business, more than enough to support a family of four, no debts, two daughters in college, but that's it. Nothing to spare for extras. They should never have sent me to a school like that. It was a recipe for heartache. I had to listen to them grumble to me every time the school asked for a contribution, and I was always scared to death I'd run out of money if I went out with my classmates and they wanted to eat someplace expensive. It's a miserable way to live."

"你能明白吧。附近的人都来这儿买，有些人来了好多年了。我们送货上门。生意不错，足够养活一家四口，没有负债，两个女儿上大学——但就这样了。没有多余的钱。他们当初就不该送我去那种学校。那是自找心碎。学校每次要捐款，我都得听他们抱怨。跟同学出去如果她们想去贵的地方吃饭，我总担心钱不够花。这种活法太难受了。"

"Hey, tell me, what d'you think the best thing is about being rich?"

"喂，你说，有钱最好的一点是什么？"

"Being able to say you don't have any money. Like, if I suggested to a classmate we do something, she could say, 'Sorry, I don't have any money.' Which is something I could never say if the situation was reversed. If I said 'I don't have any money,' it would really mean 'I don't have any money.' It's sad. Like if a pretty girl says 'I look terrible today, I don't want to go out,' that's O.K., but if an ugly girl says the same thing people laugh at her. That's what the world was like for me."

"就是可以说'我没钱'。比如我建议同学去做什么，她可以说'对不起，我没钱'。但反过来，我永远说不出这话。如果我说'我没钱'，那真的意味着我没钱。很悲哀。就像漂亮女孩说'我今天太难看了，不想出门'——这没关系；但丑女说同样的话，别人会笑她。我的世界就是这样。"

✦ 贫穷的自尊

"有钱最好的一点就是可以说'我没钱'。"——绿子的阶级意识清醒而苦涩。然而她以惊人的意志力冲破了这个牢笼。

周日的拜访
Sunday Visit · Daffodils, Shutters, and a Feast
场景 日常

SUNDAY MORNING I GOT UP AT NINE, shaved, did my laundry, and hung the clothes on the roof. It was a beautiful day. The first smell of autumn was in the air. Red dragonflies were flitting around the quadrangle, chased by neighborhood kids swinging nets. With no wind, the Rising Sun hung limp on its pole. I put on a freshly ironed shirt and walked from the dorm to the streetcar stop. A student neighborhood on a Sunday morning: the streets were dead, virtually empty, most stores closed. What few sounds there were echoed with special clarity. A flower store was open, so I went in and bought some daffodils. Daffodils in the autumn: that was strange. But I had always liked that particular flower.

周日早上我九点起床，刮了胡子，洗了衣服，晾在屋顶上。天气很好，空气中有初秋的气息。红色的蜻蜓在校园里飞舞，附近的孩子挥着捕虫网追着它们跑。没有风，太阳旗无力地垂在旗杆上。我穿上刚熨好的衬衫，从宿舍走到电车站。周日早晨的学生街区：街上死寂，几乎空无一人，大多数店铺关着门。仅有的一些声音格外清晰。有家花店开着，我进去买了一束水仙。秋天的水仙——有点奇怪，但我一直喜欢这种花。

I got off near Otsuka Station and followed Midori's map down a broad street without much to look at. None of the shops along the way seemed to be doing very well, housed as they were in old buildings with gloomy-looking interiors and faded writing on some of the signs.

我在大冢站附近下车，跟着绿子画的地图沿一条没什么看头的大路走去。沿街的店铺生意似乎都不太好，门面老旧阴沉，有些招牌的字迹已经褪色。

Ten minutes' walk down this street brought me to a corner gas station, where I turned right into a short block of shops, in the middle of which hung the sign for Kobayashi Bookstore. True, it was not a big store, but neither was it as small as Midori's description had led me to imagine. It was just a typical neighborhood bookstore, the same kind I used to run to on the very day the boys' magazines came out. A nostalgic mood overtook me as I stood in front of the place.

沿着这条路走了十分钟，来到一个转角加油站，右转进入一排短小的商店街，正中挂着"小林书店"的招牌。确实不大，但也不像绿子描述得那么小——就是那种典型的社区书店，跟我小时候男生杂志一出就去抢着买的那种一模一样。站在店门口，我涌起一阵怀旧的情绪。

The whole front of the store was sealed off by a big, roll-down metal shutter inscribed with a magazine advertisement: "Weekly Bunshun Sold Here Thursdays." I still had fifteen minutes to noon, but I didn't want to kill time wandering through the block with a handful of daffodils, so I pressed the doorbell beside the shutter and stepped a few paces back to wait.

店面前方被一块大卷帘门完全封住了，门上印着杂志广告："周四有《周刊文春》出售"。离中午还有十五分钟，但我也不想拿着一束水仙在街上闲逛，于是按下卷帘门旁的门铃，后退几步等着。

"Come in," she yelled. "Lift the shutter."

"进来，"她喊道，"把卷帘门拉上去。"

The shutter made a terrific grinding noise as I raised it three feet from the ground, ducked under, and lowered it again. The shop was pitch black inside. I managed to feel my way to the back stairway, tripping over bound piles of magazines.

卷帘门发出刺耳的声响，我抬起到离地三尺高，弯腰钻进去，又把它放下来。店里一片漆黑。我摸索着走到后面的楼梯，绊到了捆好的杂志堆。

"Over here," called Midori's voice. To the right at the top of the stairs was what looked to be a dining room, and beyond that a kitchen. The house itself was old, but the kitchen seemed to have been remodeled recently with new cabinets and a bright, shiny sink and faucet. There I found Midori preparing food. She had a pot bubbling, and the smell of broiled fish filled the air.

"这边。"绿子的声音传来。楼梯顶端右侧像是餐厅，再过去是厨房。房子很旧，但厨房似乎是新装修的——崭新的橱柜、光亮的洗碗槽和水龙头。绿子正在那里准备食物。锅在咕嘟冒泡，烤鱼的香味弥漫在空气中。

I sipped my beer and focused on Midori as she went on cooking, her back to me. She worked with quick, nimble movements, handling no fewer than four cooking procedures at once. Over here she tested the taste of a boiled dish, and the next second she was at the cutting board, rat-tat-tatting, then she took something out of the refrigerator and piled it in a dish, and before I knew it she had washed a pot she was finished using. From the back, she looked like an Indian percussionist—ringing a bell, tapping a block, striking a water buffalo bone, each movement precise and economical, with perfect balance. I watched in awe.

我啜着啤酒，注视着绿子背对着我继续做饭。她动作轻快利落，同时处理着至少四道工序。这边尝尝煮菜的味道，下一秒就站在案板前笃笃笃地切菜，然后从冰箱里拿出东西装在盘子里，转眼间又把用完的锅洗了。从背后看，她像一个印度打击乐手——摇铃、敲木鱼、击水牛骨——每个动作都精准、利落、平衡完美。我看得入迷。

绿子的厨艺与家庭往事
Cooking · Bras, Egg Pans, and Family Tragedies
故事 家庭

MIDORI'S COOKING WAS FAR BETTER than I had imagined it would be, an amazing assortment of fried, pickled, boiled, and roasted dishes using eggs, mackerel, fresh greens, eggplant, mushrooms, radishes, and sesame seeds, all done in the delicate Kyoto style.

绿子的菜比我想象的好吃太多了——煎、腌、煮、烤，用鸡蛋、鲭鱼、青菜、茄子、蘑菇、萝卜和芝麻做的一大桌子菜，全是精致的京都风味。

"Well, it's kind of a long story," she said, eating a slice of fried egg. "My mother hated housework of any kind, and she almost never cooked anything. And we had the business to think about, so it was always like 'Today we're so busy, let's order out' or 'Let's just buy some croquettes at the butcher shop' and stuff. I hated that even when I was a little kid, I mean like cooking a big pot of curry and eating the same thing three days in a row. So then one day—I was in the third year of middle school—I decided I was going to cook for the family and do it right. I went to the big Kinokuniya in Shinjuku and bought the biggest, handsomest cookbook I could find, and I mastered it from cover to cover: how to choose a cutting board, how to sharpen knives, how to bone a fish, how to shave fresh bonito flakes, everything. It turned out the author of the book was from the Kansai, so all my cooking is Kansai style."

"嗯，说来话长，"她吃了一块煎蛋说，"我妈讨厌所有家务活，几乎从不做饭。店里也忙，总是'今天太忙了叫外卖吧'或者'去肉店买点炸肉饼吧'之类。我从小就讨厌这样——比如煮一大锅咖喱连吃三天。于是有一天——我初三的时候——我决定要为家人好好做饭。我去新宿的纪伊国屋书店买了最大最漂亮的一本菜谱，从头到尾学透了：怎么选砧板、怎么磨刀、怎么剔鱼骨、怎么削鲣鱼干，全都学了。结果那本书的作者是关西人，所以我的菜全是关西风味。"

"When I was in my first year of high school, I had to have an egg fryer—a long, narrow pan for making this dashimaki style of fried egg we're eating. I bought it with money I was supposed to use for a new bra. For three months I had to live with one bra. Can you believe it? I'd wash my bra at night, go crazy trying to dry it, and wear it the next day. And if it didn't dry right, I had a tragedy to deal with. The saddest thing in the world is wearing a damp bra. I'd walk around with tears pouring from my eyes. To think I was suffering this for an egg fryer!"

"我高一那年，非得买一个玉子烧锅——就是我们现在吃的这种高汤煎蛋卷用的长条锅。我用本来要买新内衣的钱买了它。三个月只能穿一个胸罩。你能信吗？晚上洗了胸罩，拼命弄干，第二天穿。如果没干透，简直就是悲剧。世界上最悲哀的事就是穿湿胸罩。我走着走着眼泪就掉下来了。想想吧，我受这份罪就为了一个玉子烧锅！"

"I know I shouldn't say this, but actually it was kind of a relief to me when my mother died. I could run the family budget my way. I could buy what I liked. So now I've got a relatively complete set of cooking utensils. My father doesn't know a thing about the budget."

"我知道不该这么说，但说实话，我妈去世时我反而松了口气。我可以按自己的方式管钱了。想买什么就买什么。所以现在我有一套比较完整的厨具。我爸对钱的事一概不知。"

"When did your mother die?"

"你妈妈什么时候去世的？"

"Two years ago. Cancer. Brain tumor. She was in the hospital a year and a half. It was terrible. She suffered from beginning to end. Finally lost her mind, had to be doped up all the time, and still she couldn't die, though when she did it was practically a mercy killing. It's the worst kind of death—the person's in agony, the family goes through hell. It took every cent we had."

"两年了。癌症。脑瘤。住院一年半。太可怕了。从头到尾都在受苦。最后神志不清了，一直靠药物撑着，但还是死不了——死了反而是解脱。最糟糕的死法——病人受折磨，家人也煎熬。花光了所有积蓄。"

"And then there's my father," she said after some time had gone by.

"还有我爸。"过了一会儿她说。

"He went off to Uruguay in June of last year and he's been there ever since."

"他去年六月去了乌拉圭，一直待在那儿。"

"He was thinking of settling there, believe it or not. An old army buddy of his has a farm there. All of a sudden, my father announces he's going to go too, that there's no limit to what he can do in Uruguay, and he gets on a plane and that's that. We tried hard to stop him, like, 'Why do you want to go to a place like that? You can't speak the language, you've hardly ever left Tokyo.' But he wouldn't listen. Losing my mother was a real shock to him. I mean, it made him a little cuckoo. That's how much he loved her. Really."

"他打算在那儿定居，信不信由你。他有个老战友在那里有个农场。他突然就宣布也要去——说乌拉圭有无限可能，上了飞机就走了。我们拼命拦他：'你去那种地方干什么？语言也不通，你都没怎么离开过东京。'但他说不听。失去我妈对他打击太大了。都把他弄疯了。他就有那么爱她。真的。"

"What do you think he said to my sister and me when our mother died? 'I would much rather have lost the two of you than her.' It knocked the wind out of me. I couldn't say a word. You know what I mean? You just can't say something like that. O.K., he lost the woman he loved, his partner for life. I understand the pain, the sadness, the heartbreak. I pity him. But you don't tell the daughters you fathered 'You should have died in her place.' I mean, that's just too terrible. Don't you agree?"

"你知道我妈去世时他对我和我姐说了什么吗？'我宁愿失去你们两个也不愿失去她。'我当时噎住了，一句话也说不出来。你明白吗？这种话不能说的啊。好吧，他失去了他爱的女人，他一生的伴侣。我理解那种痛苦、悲伤、心碎。我同情他。但你对你亲生的女儿说'你们俩替她去死就好了'——这也太过分了。你不觉得吗？"

✦ 破碎的家庭

玉子烧锅 vs 胸罩——绿子用黑色幽默讲述自己的成长史。母亲死于癌症，父亲因悲痛过度而去了乌拉圭，留下"你们替她死就好了"的诅咒。

火灾与草莓蛋糕
Fire and Strawberry Shortcake · Rooftop, Smoke, and a Kiss
亲密 对话

ONE STRANGE THING after another came up that Sunday afternoon. A fire broke out near Midori's house and when we went up to the third-floor laundry deck to watch, we sort of kissed. It sounds stupid when I put it that way, but that was how things worked out.

那个周日下午发生了一连串奇怪的事。绿子家附近着火了，我们上三楼晒衣台去看的时候——接吻了。这样说听起来很蠢，但事情就是这样。

"It's Sakamoto's place," said Midori, leaning over the railing. "They used to make traditional door fittings and stuff. They went out of business, though."

"是坂本家，"绿子探出栏杆说，"以前做传统门窗什么的。不过已经倒闭了。"

"Hey, maybe you should gather your valuables together and get ready to evacuate this place," I said to Midori. "The wind's blowing the other way now, but it could change any time, and you've got a gas station right there. I'll help you pack."

"喂，你也许该收拾一下贵重物品准备撤离，"我对绿子说，"现在风往那边吹，但随时可能转向，而且你们旁边就是加油站。我帮你打包。"

"Forget it. I'm not running away."

"算了，我不逃。"

"Even if this place burns?"

"就算这里烧起来？"

"You heard me. I don't mind dying."

"我说了，我不介意死。"

Midori brought two floor pillows, four cans of beer, and a guitar from downstairs. We drank and watched the black smoke rising. Midori strummed and sang. I asked her if she didn't think this might anger the neighbors. Drinking beer and singing while you watched a local fire from the laundry deck didn't seem like the most admirable behavior I could think of.

绿子从楼下拿来两个座垫、四罐啤酒和一把吉他。我们喝着酒，看着黑烟升腾。绿子弹着吉他唱歌。我问她这样会不会惹邻居生气——在晒衣台上喝着啤酒唱歌看火灾，似乎说不上光彩的行为。

She sang some of the folk songs she had played with her group. … After she had gone through most of the songs she knew, she sang an old one that she said she had written herself.

她唱了一些以前和乐队唱过的民谣……唱完大部分会唱的歌之后，她又唱了一首据说是她自己写的旧歌。

I'd love to cook a stew for you / But I have no pot. / I'd love to knit a scarf for you / But I have no wool. / I'd love to write a poem for you / But I have no pen.

我想为你炖锅汤 / 但我没有锅。 / 我想为你织条围巾 / 但我没有毛线。 / 我想为你写首诗 / 但我没有笔。

"It's called 'I Have Nothing,'" Midori announced. It was a truly terrible song, both words and music.

"叫《我一无所有》。"绿子介绍说。这首歌从词到曲都难听得要命。

Tired of singing, Midori put her guitar down and slumped against my shoulder like a cat in the sun.

唱累了，绿子放下吉他，像晒太阳的猫一样靠在我肩膀上。

"You know," she said, "when my mother died … I didn't feel the least bit sad. … And I didn't feel sad when my father left, either."

"你知道吗，"她说，"我妈去世的时候……我一点都不难过。……我爸走的时候，我也不难过。"

"I always thought, I mean, they're my mother and father, of course I'd be sad if they died or I never saw them again. But it didn't happen that way. I didn't feel anything. Not sad, not lonely. I hardly even think of them. Sometimes I'll have dreams, though. Sometimes my mother will be glaring at me out of the darkness and she'll accuse me of being happy she died. But I'm not happy she died. I'm just not very sad. And to tell the truth, I never shed a single tear. I cried all night when my cat died, though, when I was little."

"我一直以为——我是说，他们是我的父母——如果他们死了或我再也不见他们了，我当然会难过。但事实并非如此。我一点感觉也没有。不难过，也不寂寞。我几乎都不怎么想他们。但有时会做梦。有时我妈从黑暗里瞪着我，指责我因为她的死而高兴。但我没有因为她的死而高兴。我只是不怎么难过而已。说实话，我一滴眼泪也没掉过。不过小时候我的猫死的时候，我哭了一整夜。"

"But I'm not the only one to blame," Midori continued. "It's true I've got a cold streak. I recognize that. But if they—my father and mother—had loved me a little more, I would have been able to feel more—to feel real sadness, for example."

"但也不全是我的错，"绿子继续说，"我确实有冷漠的一面，我承认。但如果他们——我爸和我妈——多爱我一点，我就能感受到更多——比如真正的悲伤。"

"I was always hungry for love. Just once, I wanted to know what it was like to get my fill of it—to be fed so much love I couldn't take any more. Just once. But they never gave that to me. Never, not once. If I tried to cuddle up and beg for something, they'd just shove me away and yell at me. 'No! That costs too much!' It's all I ever heard. So I made up my mind I was going to find someone who would love me unconditionally three hundred and sixty-five days a year. I was still in elementary school at the time—fifth or sixth grade—but I made up my mind once and for all."

"我一直渴望被爱。哪怕一次也好，我想知道被爱填满是什么感觉——被爱到吃不下了。就一次。但他们从未给过我。一次也没有。如果我撒娇讨要什么，他们就把我推开，冲我大喊：'不行！太贵了！'我听到的永远只有这个。所以那时我就下定了决心——要找一个一年三百六十五天无条件爱我的人。我当时还在上小学，五六年级——但我一旦下定决心就不会改变。"

"I guess I've been waiting so long I'm looking for perfection. That makes it tough."

"大概等得太久，我开始追求完美了。这就难了。"

"I'm looking for selfishness. Perfect selfishness. Like, say I tell you I want to eat strawberry shortcake. And you stop everything you're doing and run out and buy it for me. And you come back out of breath and get down on your knees and hold this strawberry shortcake out to me. And I say I don't want it anymore and throw it out the window. That's what I'm looking for."

"我在寻找自私——完美的自私。比如，我告诉你我想吃草莓奶油蛋糕。你放下一切跑出去给我买。你气喘吁吁地回来，跪下来把草莓奶油蛋糕捧到我面前。然后我说我不想吃了，把它扔出窗外。这就是我在找的。"

"I'm not sure that has anything to do with love," I said with some amazement.

"我不确定这和爱有什么关系。"我有些惊讶地说。

"It does," she said. "You just don't know it. There are times in a girl's life when things like that are incredibly important."

"有关系的，"她说，"你不懂而已。女孩子一生中，有些时候这种事重要得不得了。"

She looked into my eyes, and I into hers. I put my arm around her and kissed her. The slightest twinge went through her shoulders, and then she relaxed and closed her eyes for several seconds. The early autumn sun cast the shadow of her lashes on her cheek, and I could see it trembling in outline.

她凝视着我的眼睛，我也凝视着她的。我伸手搂住她，吻了她。她的肩膀微微一抽，然后放松下来，闭上眼睛几秒钟。初秋的阳光将她的睫毛影投射在脸颊上，我可以看到那轮廓在微微颤动。

It was a soft and gentle kiss, one not meant to lead beyond itself. I would probably not have kissed Midori that day if we hadn't spent the afternoon on the laundry deck in the sun, drinking beer and watching a fire, and she probably felt the same. After a long time of watching the glittering rooftops and the smoke and the red dragonflies and other things, we had felt something warm and close, and we both probably wanted, half-consciously, to preserve that mood in some form. It was that kind of kiss. But as with all kisses, it was not without a certain element of danger.

那是一个温柔轻柔的吻，没有打算更进一步。如果不是那天下午我们在晒衣台上晒太阳、喝啤酒、看火灾，我大概不会吻绿子——她大概也一样。凝望了很久闪光的天台、烟雾、红色蜻蜓和其他东西之后，我们感觉到了某种温暖而靠近的东西，两人大概都半自觉地想把那种氛围以某种形式保存下来。就是那种吻。但和所有的吻一样，它并非没有危险成分。

✦ "完美的自私"

"我在寻找自私——完美的自私。"买了草莓蛋糕却扔掉它，而男人要为此道歉。绿子的爱情哲学匪夷所思，却又令人动容。屋顶上的吻——秋天、黑烟、啤酒和她写的难听的歌。

黎明与来信
Dawn and the Letter · Shinjuku, Strangers, Naoko
孤独 夜游

The first to speak was Midori. She held my hand and told me, with what seemed like some difficulty, that she was seeing someone. I said that I had sensed as much.

先开口的是绿子。她握着我的手，有些艰难地告诉我她在和一个人交往。我说我猜到了。

"Do you have a girl you like?" she asked.

"你有喜欢的女孩吗？"她问。

"I do," I said.

"有。"我说。

"But you're always free on Sundays, right?"

"但你周日总是有空，对吧？"

"It's very complicated," I said.

"很复杂。"我说。

And then I realized that the brief spell of the early autumn afternoon had vanished.

然后我意识到，初秋午后那段短暂的魔咒已经消逝了。

Nagasawa came to my room that Saturday afternoon and suggested we have one of our nights on the town. He would arrange an overnight pass for me. I said I would go. I had been feeling especially foggy-brained for the past week and was ready to sleep with anybody, it didn't matter much who.

那个周六下午，永泽来到我的房间，提议出去找乐子。他可以帮我弄到外宿许可。我说好。过去一周我一直头昏脑涨，已经准备好和任何人睡了——跟谁都行。

When eleven-thirty rolled around, Nagasawa was ready to give up. "Sorry I dragged you around for nothing," he said.

十一点半时，永泽准备放弃了。"抱歉让你白跑一趟。"他说。

In fact, I didn't care about getting laid anymore. Wandering around Shinjuku on a noisy Saturday night, observing the mysterious energy created by a mix of sex and alcohol, I began to feel that my own desire was a puny thing.

其实我早已不在乎能不能泡到谁了。在喧嚣的周六夜晚游荡新宿，观察着性与酒精混合产生的神秘能量，我感到自己的欲望渺小得很。

I had had a number of unusual experiences in Shinjuku, but I had never before been invited to have a drink with two strange girls at five-twenty in the morning. Refusing would have been more trouble than it was worth, and time was no problem, so I bought an armload of sake and snacks from a nearby machine, and the three of us went to an empty lot by the west exit of the station to hold an impromptu drinking party.

我在新宿经历过不少离奇的事，但从没有在清晨五点二十分被两个陌生女孩邀请喝酒。拒绝比答应还麻烦，而且我有的是时间，于是从附近的自动售货机买了一堆清酒和零食，三个人到车站西出口旁的空地上开起了临时酒会。

She was gone when I woke at twelve-thirty. I found no note of any kind. One side of my head felt strangely heavy from having drunk at an odd time. I took a shower to wake myself up, shaved, and sat in a chair, naked, drinking a bottle of juice from the refrigerator and reviewing in order the events of the night before. Each scene felt unreal and strangely distanced, as if I were viewing it through two or three layers of glass, but the events had undoubtedly happened to me.

十二点半醒来时她已经走了。什么留言也没留。我的半边脑袋因为在不正常的时间喝酒而异常沉重。我冲了澡醒神，刮了胡子，赤裸着坐在椅子上，喝了一瓶冰箱里的果汁，按顺序回忆昨晚发生的一切。每个场景都感觉不真实，像隔着两三层玻璃一样奇怪地遥远，但那些事确实发生在我身上。

I ate a light lunch in Shinjuku and went to a telephone booth to call Midori Kobayashi on the off chance that she might be home alone waiting for a call again today. I let it ring fifteen times but no one answered. I tried again twenty minutes later with the same result. Then I took a bus back to the dorm. A special delivery letter was waiting for me in the mailbox by the entry. It was from Naoko.

我在新宿吃了简单的午餐，然后去电话亭试着打给小林绿子——说不定她又一个人在家等电话。让电话响了十五声，没人接。二十分钟后又打了一次，还是没人接。然后我坐巴士回宿舍。入口信箱里有一封快递信在等着我——来自直子。

✦ 两个世界之间

白天的吻属于绿子，夜晚的陌生女人属于空洞——而信箱里等待着他的，是直子的信。渡边在两个截然不同的世界之间漂移。
